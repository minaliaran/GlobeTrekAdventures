About the City

Polonnaruwa is one of Sri Lanka's most important ancient cities and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, known for its well-preserved ruins and remarkable archaeological heritage.

Once the island's second capital after Anuradhapura, the city is home to grand palaces, massive reservoirs, and iconic landmarks such as Gal Vihara, showcasing exquisite stone carvings of the Buddha. Its historical depth and architectural brilliance make it a must-visit destination.

Key Sites

Polonnaruwa Highlights

Gal Vihara Rock Temple

Four majestic Buddha statues carved out of a single enormous granite rock face.

Polonnaruwa Vatadage

An intricate circular relic house featuring the finest sandakada pahana and guard stones.

Parakrama Samudra

The 'Sea of Parakrama,' a massive man-made reservoir that still sustains the region.

Rankoth Vehera

The largest stupa in Polonnaruwa, built to mimic the scale of the great Ruwanwelisaya.

Archaeological Museum

A gallery showcasing recovered bronzes and stone masterpieces of the medieval era.
