Engineering Masterpiece

Parakrama Samudra

The 'Sea of Parakrama,' a colossal 12th-century reservoir that stands as a testament to the hydraulic genius of ancient Sri Lanka.

Hydraulics & Life

The Ancient Sea

Built by King Parakramabahu I, who famously decreed that 'not a single drop of rain should flow into the sea without being used for the benefit of man,' the Parakrama Samudra consists of five separate tanks joined together. Covering over 2,200 hectares, it is one of the largest irrigation projects of the ancient world.

The reservoir still provides water for thousands of acres of rice cultivation, sustaining the livelihoods of modern farmers just as it did for the subjects of the medieval kingdom. The massive 12-kilometer dam wall (bund) is a popular spot for sunset walks, offering sweeping views of the water and the distant mountains.
