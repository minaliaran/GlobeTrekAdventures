Udawalawe Dam & Reservoir

Discover the massive man-made lake that sustains the surrounding wildlife and agriculture, offering stunning views of the highlands across its surface.

Nature & Infrastructure

The Gentle Life-Force

The Udawalawe Dam, constructed in the late 1960s, is one of the largest irrigation projects in Sri Lanka. It was built across the Walawe River, creating a vast reservoir that spans approximately 3,400 hectares. While its primary purpose is electricity generation and agriculture, it has become the central life-force for the Udawalawe National Park.

Driving across the dam wall provides a spectacular panoramic view of the region. On one side, the calm expanse of the reservoir stretches toward the backdrop of the Sabaragamuwa mountains; on the other, the river flows down toward the southern plains. It is a perfect place for bird watching, especially for pelicans, storks, and eagles that hunt across the massive surface of the water.

Explore The Dam

  • Best View:
    The road along the top of the dam wall offers the best 360-degree views of the area.
  • Sunset Spot:
    The dam area is one of the most beautiful places in the region to watch the sunset over the water.
  • Access:
    No separate ticket is required for the dam road. It is a public thoroughfare and very safe.
  • Wildlife:
    Keep an eye out for elephants often grazing near the edges of the water during a late-afternoon safari.
