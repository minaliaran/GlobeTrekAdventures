Solosmasthana Site

Muthiyangana Viharaya

One of the sixteen most sacred Buddhist sites in Sri Lanka, nestled in the heart of Badulla town with a history of over 2,500 years.

Spiritual Heritage

The Sacred Heart of Uva

The Muthiyangana Raja Maha Vihara is a site of immense spiritual and historic importance. It is one of the "Solosmasthana," the sixteen places sanctified by the visit of Lord Buddha to the island. According to legend, the Buddha arrived here on his third visit to Sri Lanka, at the invitation of the local King Maniakkhika.

The central stupa is believed to enshrine sacred hair and bone relics of the Buddha, as well as a "Muthi" (pearl) which gives the temple its name. The complex features beautiful ancient murals, a sacred Bodhi tree, and a serene atmosphere that offers a peaceful retreat from the surrounding city. For centuries, it has been the primary center of worship for the people of the Uva highlands, standing as a timeless testament to the island's deep-rooted faith.

Quick Facts

  • Antiquity:
    The site has been a center of worship since the time of the Buddha, over 2,500 years ago.
  • Pilgrimage:
    A vital stop on any pilgrimage through the hill country of Sri Lanka.
  • Dress Code:
    Modest, white-colored attire is traditionally requested when visiting the shrine.
