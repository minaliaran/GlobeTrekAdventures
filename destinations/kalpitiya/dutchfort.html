Historical Heritage

Kalpitiya Dutch Fort

Built by the Dutch in the 17th century, this historic fortification stands as a testament to the colonial trade wars for control of the peninsula.

Centuries of Strategy

The Colonial Guardian

Kalpitiya Dutch Fort was a critical strategic point during the colonial era, designed to protect and control the sea trade in the North Western Province. The fort, though now partially in ruins, still exhibits the imposing architecture of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), featuring thick stone walls and a unique four-bastion layout.

Walking through the fort's grounds reveals remnants of barracks, ancient storage rooms, and a historic church within its walls. Exploring this site offers a fascinating insight into the colonial history of the island, where Portuguese, Dutch, and eventually British interests intersected on this remote, strategically vital peninsula.

Quick Facts

  • Military Presence:
    The fort is currently occupied by the Sri Lanka Navy, so permissions are sometimes required.
  • Identification:
    Always carry legal ID (passport or NIC) when visiting military-administered sites.
  • Era:
    The current stone fort was completed in 1676, built over an earlier Portuguese site.
