Koneswaram Temple

Explore the ancient Hindu temple perched on Swami Rock, a spiritual landmark with dramatic ocean views and thousands of years of history.

Faith & History

The Temple of a Thousand Pillars

Koneswaram Temple (also known as the Dakshana Kailasam) is one of the five 'Ishwarams' or sacred Hindu temples dedicated to Lord Shiva in Sri Lanka. It handles a significant place in Hindu mythology and history, built atop Swami Rock, a vertical cliff that drops over 100 meters into the Indian Ocean.

The temple site has been a major religious center for over two millennia. While much of the original structure was destroyed during the colonial era, the spirit of the place remains unbroken. Visiting Koneswaram is a unique spiritual experience; the temple complex is vibrant with colors, traditional music, and the scent of jasmine and incense. The location also offers the famous 'Lovers' Leap' and breathtaking vistas of the Trincomalee harbor.

Temple Protocol

  • Dress Code:
    Modest clothing is essential. Shoulders and knees must be covered. Footwear must be removed at the entrance.
  • Pooja Timings:
    Main rituals (poojas) happen early morning and late afternoon. These are the most vibrant times to visit.
  • Photography:
    Photos are allowed in the outer complex, but strictly forbidden inside the inner sanctum.
  • Deer Sanctuary:
    Spotted deer roam freely around the walkway to the temple. Be respectful of them and their habitat.
