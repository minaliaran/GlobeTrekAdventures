Site of the First Royal Wedding

Magul Maha Viharaya

Explore the ancient ruins where King Kavantissa and Viharamahadevi were historically married, a sanctuary of royal history hidden in the south.

Archeology & Royalty

The Royal Union

Magul Maha Viharaya is an ancient Buddhist temple complex located in Pottuvil, near Yala. Its name, 'Magul', translates to 'Marriage', reflecting the historical belief that this was the location where King Kavantissa married Princess Viharamahadevi after her miraculous landing at Kirinda. The ruins are some of the best-preserved examples of Anuradhapura-style architecture in the south of the island.

The site features a massive stupa, a beautiful stone moonstone (unique for its depiction of mahouts on the elephants), and several image houses surrounded by large, ancient trees. It's a remarkably peaceful location, often overshadowed by the larger national parks, providing a quiet space for visitors to reflect on the stories of the kings and queens who once walked these grounds.

Site Knowledge

  • Moonstone:
    Check the rare 'Magul Maha' moonstone, which is different from all others in Sri Lanka due to its carving details.
  • Dress Code:
    Modest attire is required as this remains a sacred Buddhist site. Remove shoes before entering the stupa area.
  • Surroundings:
    The site is surrounded by dense jungle. It's often very quiet and perfect for meditative walks.
  • Location:
    Located on the road from Pottuvil to Monaragala, about 45 minutes northeast of main Yala town.
