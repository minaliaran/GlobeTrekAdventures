Wetlands & Birds

The Vibrant Lagoons

Bundala National Park was the first wetland in Sri Lanka to be designated as a Ramsar site and is also a UNESCO biosphere reserve. It's an essential wintering ground for several migratory birds. The park is famous for its complex system of lagoons and scrubs, supporting over 200 bird species, including large flocks of Greater Flamingo that arrive during the migratory season.

While birds are the stars of the show, Bundala also hosts Asian elephants, marsh crocodiles, and various species of monkeys and deer. Its landscape is starkly beautiful, with salt pans, sandy dunes, and thick marshlands. It offers a quieter, more intimate safari experience compared to the busy trails of neighboring Yala, making it a perfect retreat for nature purists and bird-watching enthusiasts.

Surveyor Insights

  • Migration Season:
    September to March is the best time for bird watching, as migratory birds flee the northern winter.
  • Key Spot:
    The lagoons are the primary areas for spotting Flamingos, Pelicans, and Storks.
  • Best Time:
    Early morning (06:00 AM) is the best for catching the birds in flight and active feeding.
  • Equipment:
    High-zoom binoculars or a telephoto camera lens are essential for a good experience here.
